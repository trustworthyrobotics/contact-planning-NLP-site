Lifted Multiple Shooting for Parallel-in-Time Contact-Rich Trajectory Optimization

Wei-Chen Li, Glen Chou
Georgia Institute of Technology
Paper Code arXiv

Abstract

Planning through contact requires accounting for the coupled dynamics of a robot and its environment, including inertial effects, contact forces, and friction. However, the nonsmoothness and multimodality of contact dynamics and contact geometry makes gradient-based optimization challenging. In this work, we propose lifted multiple shooting, a trajectory optimization transcription that can be solved directly using nonlinear program solvers. By introducing contact impulses as optimization variables, our formulation allows the optimizer to satisfy the state-transition constraints using friction-cone-feasible impulses while separately enforcing their consistency with the contact model. Our formulation supports a broad class of contact constitutive relations, with sensitivities computed through implicit differentiation. To enable efficient planning over long horizons and for high-dimensional systems, we further introduce IP-PAS, a parallel-in-time primal-dual interior-point solver that achieves \(O(\log T)\) parallel computational depth. We evaluate the proposed methods on six fully dynamic contact-rich planning tasks, including nonprehensile pushing and in-hand manipulation, without contact-guiding costs. Lifted multiple shooting improves success rates by up to 81.5 percentage points over standard multiple shooting, while IP-PAS achieves solution quality comparable to serial methods with up to a 3.1× speedup and scales to horizons of 40,000 timesteps.

Mathematical transcription

Planning through contact couples the robot’s motion to the impulses it exchanges with the environment. The four transcriptions differ in which variables the optimizer can adjust and how it enforces contact.

One step of contact dynamics

Let \(x=(q,v)\) be the current state, with configuration \(q\) and velocity \(v\). The control is \(u\), the contact impulse is \(\gamma\), and the next state is \(x^+\).

\(f_\text{contact}\)
Solves the contact-resolution problem for the current state and control to obtain the contact impulse.
\(f_\text{integrate}\)
Advances the dynamics by one time step using the current state, control, and supplied contact impulse.
\(f_\text{step}\)
Computes the next state from the current state and control by resolving contact first and then integrating.
\[ \begin{aligned} x^+ &= f_\text{integrate}(x,u,\gamma), \\ \gamma &= f_\text{contact}(x,u), \\ x^+ &= f_\text{step}(x,u) := f_\text{integrate}\bigl(x,u,f_\text{contact}(x,u)\bigr). \end{aligned} \]

Single shooting transcription

Optimize only the controls. Roll out \(x_{t+1} = f_\text{step}(x_t,u_t)\) from the initial condition \(\hat{x}_0\).

\[ \min_{u_{0:T-1}} \ \textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t,u_t) + \ell_T(x_T) \]

Multiple shooting transcription

Optimize the states and controls. Enforce the dynamics between adjacent states as equality constraints.

\[ \min_{x_{0:T},u_{0:T-1}} \ \textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t,u_t) + \ell_T(x_T) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{s.t.}\quad & x_0 = \hat{x}_0, \\ & x_{t+1} = f_\text{step}(x_t,u_t), \quad t=0,\dots,T-1. \end{aligned} \]

Contact implicit transcription

Optimize states, controls, and impulses. Enforce the contact solver’s complementarity conditions in the trajectory optimization.

\[ \min_{\substack{x_{0:T}, \\ u_{0:T-1}, \\ \gamma_{0:T-1}}} \ \textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t,u_t) + \ell_T(x_T) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{s.t.}\quad & x_0 = \hat{x}_0, \\ & x_{t+1} = f_\text{integrate}(x_t,u_t,\gamma_t), \quad t=0,\dots,T-1, \\ & \mathcal{F}_\mu \ni \gamma_t \perp J(q_t)\,v_{t+1} + [\begin{matrix} \tfrac{\phi(q_t)}{\delta t} & 0 & 0 \end{matrix}]^\top \in \mathcal{F}_\mu^*, \\ & t=0,\dots,T-1. \end{aligned} \]

Lifted multiple shooting transcription

Optimize states, controls, and impulses. Keep the dynamics, contact consistency, and friction-cone feasibility as separate constraints.

\[ \min_{\substack{x_{0:T},\,u_{0:T-1}, \\ \gamma_{0:T-1}}} \ \textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \ell(x_t,u_t) + \ell_T(x_T) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{s.t.}\quad & x_0 = \hat{x}_0, \\ & x_{t+1} = f_\text{integrate}(x_t,u_t,\gamma_t), \quad t=0,\dots,T-1, \\ & \gamma_t = f_\text{contact}(x_t,u_t), \quad t=0,\dots,T-1, \\ & \gamma_t \in \mathcal{F}_\mu, \quad t=0,\dots,T-1. \end{aligned} \]

\(\mathcal{F}_\mu\) is the friction cone and \(\mathcal{F}_\mu^*\) is its dual. The contact Jacobian \(J\) maps velocity into contact coordinates, \(\phi\) is the contact gap, and \(\delta t\) is the time step.

Interior point - Parallel associative scan

At each interior-point iteration, IP-PAS solves the linearized Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) equations of the nonlinear program (NLP) for the lifted multiple shooting transcription. Parallel associative scans compute the primal–dual update across time by processing each level of a binary tree in parallel.

The five-tuple below describes the state and costate relationship between stages i and j.

\[ \mathcal{E}_{i\to j} = (P_{ij},p_{ij},\bar{A}_{ij},b_{ij},C_{ij}) \]

The tuples have a composition rule:

\[ \mathcal{E}_{i\to j} \circ \mathcal{E}_{j\to k} = \mathcal{E}_{i\to k} \]

The composition rule is associative.

The postfix scan gives the backward costate relationships. IP-PAS then uses a separate forward prefix scan to recover state updates from the initial condition.

Results

The paper evaluates six manipulation tasks with 200 initial conditions per task and solver. A trial succeeds when its terminal goal error is below 0.01. Reported solve times include dynamics evaluations.
